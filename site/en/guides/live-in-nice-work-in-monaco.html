You are starting a job or an assignment in Monaco. You need somewhere to live for the coming months, before you settle for good or for the length of your contract.

Living in Nice is the choice of one in four private-sector employees in Monaco. This guide explains how to live in Nice and work in Monaco, and how to find a home for 1 to 10 months.

01How many Monaco employees live in Nice?

According to the IMSEE 2025 Employment Observatory, Monaco's statistics institute, more than 16,000 private-sector employees in Monaco live in Nice. That is one worker in four. The figures were reported by Monaco Tribune on 7 May 2026.

So you join a commute that thousands of people make every day.

02How do you get from Nice to Monaco?

By train. The TER links Nice and Monaco Monte-Carlo. According to SNCF TER Sud, the line runs 65 return trips a day, with an average journey of 20 minutes.

By car. From the Les Piafs des Collines apartment, Monaco is 22 minutes away, outside traffic.

By bus and tram. Bus 60 stops 50 m from the home. It connects with tram line 2 at Magnan, which serves central Nice, including the Jean Médecin stop.

A private parking space within the grounds is included in the monthly price. If you combine car and train, your vehicle stays within the villa's enclosed grounds.

03What formalities are needed to work in Monaco?

Hiring an employee in Monaco goes through the employer. According to the official portal monentreprise.gouv.mc:

  • the employer files its job offer with the Service de l'Emploi;
  • it fills in, with the employee, the "Demande d'autorisation d'embauchage et de permis de travail";
  • once approvals are granted, the employee receives a work permit and social security registration numbers.

Your future employer knows this procedure. Ask for the timetable, it often sets your arrival date.

04What about taxes?

Your tax situation depends on your tax residence, assessed under article 4 B du code général des impôts, and on your nationality. Tax relations between France and Monaco are governed by the convention signed on 18 May 1963.

Your local tax office (service des impôts des particuliers) or a tax adviser will tell you what applies to your case.

05Which contract for a home in Nice?

There are two routes, depending on who pays.

You pay the rent. You sign a *bail mobilité*, a French furnished lease of 1 to 10 months. It is provided for by articles 25-12 et suivants de la loi n° 89-462 du 6 juillet 1989, introduced by the loi ELAN du 23 novembre 2018.

  • Length of 1 to 10 months.
  • Neither renewable nor automatically extended.
  • No security deposit.
  • Reason to prove before signing: professional transfer, temporary assignment, professional training, internship, among others.

Your transfer letter or your assignment order serves as proof. If you are hired by a new employer in Monaco, the reason is checked before signing: the law covers professional transfer and temporary assignment, not hiring as such. In that case, the lease in your employer's name, from 3 months, requires no proof of reason.

Your employer pays. A Monaco company, a relocation agency or an insurer can rent in its own name. The lease is in the organisation's name, from 3 months, with an invoice in its name. No proof of reason is required.

06What guarantee?

Visale is not available at this rent level. The guarantee takes the form of a guarantor: your employer or a private individual.

08Why a transition home?

Finding a long-term home takes time: viewings, application, guarantees. A lease of 1 to 10 months lets you start your job without waiting.

You discover the neighbourhoods, you test your commutes, then you choose where to settle next.
